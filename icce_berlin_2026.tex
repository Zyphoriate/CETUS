\begin{abstract}
Predicting smartphone battery Time-to-Empty (TTE) under realistic workloads is challenging because power demand, electrochemical state, temperature, and aging all interact. We present a continuous-time framework that couples a second-order Thevenin equivalent-circuit model (ECM) and an Open-Circuit Voltage to State-of-Charge (OCV--SOC) lookup with a system-level load model aggregating screen, CPU, cellular, WiFi, GPS and background contributions. A constant-power load constraint is enforced through an implicit per-step current solve, and a debounced cutoff stabilises the shutdown decision. Capacity and resistance temperature dependencies are calibrated from public battery datasets, and component power coefficients are estimated from Android Debug Bridge (ADB) traces of a 4724~mAh device. Across four representative usage profiles at \SI{25}{\celsius} the predicted TTE spans 39.45~h (idle), 9.06~h (video), 5.34~h (gaming) and 4.08~h (mixed). Sensitivity analysis identifies screen brightness, network activity, CPU load and baseline draw as dominant TTE drivers, while low-power peripherals contribute marginally. The framework supports user- and OS-level battery-life recommendations and is portable to other consumer devices.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Smartphones are dominant consumer electronics platforms, and battery life remains one of the most user-visible quality attributes. Yet under nominally similar usage the runtime often varies by hours, because the battery is simultaneously a load-driven electrical system, a thermally coupled cell, and a slowly aging electrochemical device. Prior work has addressed each piece in isolation: equivalent circuit models (ECMs) capture short-term electrical dynamics \cite{plett2015}, OCV--SOC characterisation provides voltage--state mappings \cite{zhu2022capacity}, temperature dependencies follow Arrhenius-type laws \cite{arrhenius1889}, and aging models capture cycle-induced fade \cite{nasa_dataset}. Component-level power profiling on Android has been studied since PowerTutor \cite{zhang2010powertutor}.

What is still missing for a consumer-electronics design context is a single, reproducible continuous-time pipeline that (i) takes a workload trace as input, (ii) solves the implicit constant-power coupling between terminal voltage and battery current, and (iii) returns Time-to-Empty (TTE) and component-resolved drain attributions consistent with both bench data and on-device measurements. This paper contributes such a pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
\item A coupled ECM--OCV--load model with quadratic per-step current solve and debounced cutoff, separating shutdown threshold from numerical feasibility.
\item A calibration workflow that fuses public temperature--capacity data \cite{en15010060}, NASA aging measurements \cite{nasa_dataset}, Zenodo OCV--SOC data \cite{zhu2022capacity} and ADB-derived component power coefficients.
\item Cross-scenario TTE predictions and an elasticity-based sensitivity analysis that ranks the drivers most worth optimising in OS scheduling and user behaviour.
\end{itemize}

\section{System Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{State-of-Charge Dynamics}
Let $z(t)\in[0,1]$ be the battery State-of-Charge (SOC). Coulomb counting gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{dz}{dt} = -\frac{I(t)}{Q_{\max,\mathrm{eff}}(T,N)},
\end{equation}
where $I(t)$ is the battery current (discharge positive), and the effective capacity factorises into a slowly varying capacity state, a temperature multiplier and an aging multiplier:
\begin{equation}
Q_{\max,\mathrm{eff}}(T,N)=Q_{\max}(t)\,\phi_{\mathrm{temp}}(T)\,\phi_{\mathrm{age}}(N,T).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Second-Order Thevenin ECM}
The terminal voltage uses ohmic resistance plus two polarisation RC branches \cite{plett2015}:
\begin{align}
V_t(t) &= V_{oc}(z(t)) - R_0(T)\,I(t) - v_1(t) - v_2(t),\\
\dot v_1 &= -\tfrac{1}{R_1 C_1}v_1 + \tfrac{1}{C_1}I,\\
\dot v_2 &= -\tfrac{1}{R_2 C_2}v_2 + \tfrac{1}{C_2}I,
\end{align}
where $V_{oc}(\cdot)$ is taken from the OCV--SOC lookup of Section~\ref{sec:data}.

\subsection{Constant-Power Load and Implicit Current Solve}
The workload trace provides battery-side load power demand $P_{\mathrm{load}}(t)$. With DC--DC efficiency $\eta_{\mathrm{dc}}$, current and voltage are coupled by
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{load}}(t) = \eta_{\mathrm{dc}}\,V_t(t)\,I(t).
\end{equation}
Because $V_t$ depends on $I$, substituting the ECM voltage produces a quadratic in $I$ at each time step:
\begin{equation}
\eta_{\mathrm{dc}}R_0(T)\,I^2 - \eta_{\mathrm{dc}}\bigl(V_{oc}(z)-v_1-v_2\bigr)I + P_{\mathrm{load}} = 0,
\label{eq:quad}
\end{equation}
and we keep the physically admissible (nonnegative) root. When the discriminant becomes infeasible at very low voltage we enforce a numerical lower bound $V_{\min}$ on the effective driving voltage. We deliberately separate this from the user-facing shutdown cutoff $V_{\mathrm{cut}}$, used only to define TTE.

\subsection{Debounced Cutoff}
Short voltage dips during sustained loads can cause premature termination. We optionally apply a first-order low-pass on the terminal voltage,
\begin{equation}
\dot v_f = \frac{V_t - v_f}{\tau_f},
\end{equation}
and declare end-of-discharge when $v_f \le V_{\mathrm{cut}}$ instead of $V_t \le V_{\mathrm{cut}}$.

\subsection{Power Consumption Model}
Total load power is decomposed by component:
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{load}}(t) = P_{\mathrm{base}} + \sum_k \alpha_k(t)\,x_k(t),
\end{equation}
where $x_k(t)$ encodes component-specific usage (e.g., screen brightness, CPU utilisation, radio activity, GPS state) and $\alpha_k(t)$ is the corresponding power coefficient. This modular form lets us swap workload profiles without changing the battery side of the model.

\subsection{Temperature and Aging}
The capacity multiplier is a clipped cubic:
\begin{equation}
\phi_{\mathrm{temp}}(T) = \mathrm{clip}\bigl(\mathrm{poly}_3(T),\phi_{\min},\phi_{\max}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and ohmic resistance follows an Arrhenius law \cite{arrhenius1889}
\begin{equation}
R_0(T) = R_{0,\mathrm{ref}}\exp\!\Bigl(\beta\bigl(\tfrac{1}{T+273.15}-\tfrac{1}{298.15}\bigr)\Bigr).
\end{equation}
A cell-level energy balance \cite{bernardi1985} gives the thermal state
\begin{equation}
m c_p \frac{dT_b}{dt} = Q_{\mathrm{gen}} - hA(T_b-T_{\mathrm{amb}}),
\end{equation}
with $Q_{\mathrm{gen}} = I^2 R_0 + v_1^2/R_1 + v_2^2/R_2$. Aging enters multiplicatively via $\phi_{\mathrm{age}}(N,T)=\exp(-k(T)N)$, with $k(T)$ fitted from cycle data.

\section{Calibration Data}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Temperature--Capacity Multiplier}
We normalise reported capacity \cite{en15010060} by the value at \SI{25}{\celsius} and fit a cubic
\begin{align}
\phi_{\mathrm{temp}}(T) =\;& -1.234\!\times\!10^{-6}\,T^{3} - 8.259\!\times\!10^{-5}\,T^{2}\nonumber\\
& + 1.225\!\times\!10^{-2}\,T + 7.359\!\times\!10^{-1}.
\end{align}
The fit (Fig.~\ref{fig:phi_ocv}\subref{fig:phi}) captures the well-known capacity drop at low temperatures and the saturation near room conditions.

\subsection{Cycle Aging}
Fitting an exponential capacity-fade law to the NASA aging set \cite{nasa_dataset} gives a temperature-dependent aging coefficient
\begin{equation}
k(T) = 3.7\!\times\!10^{-5}\,T + 1.4\!\times\!10^{-3},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ degradation accelerates monotonically with $T$, consistent with Arrhenius-type kinetics.

\section{Results and Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{sec:results}
We simulate a fully charged 4724~mAh cell at \SI{25}{\celsius} with $V_{\mathrm{cut}}=\SI{3.3}{\volt}$. The implicit current solve in \eqref{eq:quad} runs at \SI{1}{\hertz}; the debounced cutoff uses $\tau_f=\SI{30}{\second}$.

\subsection{Time-to-Empty Across Profiles}
Fig.~\ref{fig:tte_soc} reports SOC trajectories and the resulting TTE values. The model predicts 39.45~h (idle), 9.06~h (video), 5.34~h (gaming) and 4.08~h (mixed): close to an order-of-magnitude spread between idle and the heaviest mixed-use profile. The TTE ordering matches qualitative expectations: gaming combines high CPU and bright display, while \textit{mixed} adds sustained network activity.

\subsection{Temperature and Initial-SOC Effects}
Fig.~\ref{fig:tte_temp} shows TTE under the mixed profile across ambient temperatures, and Fig.~\ref{fig:tte_3d} extends this to a TTE surface over initial SOC and temperature. Cold conditions sharply compress TTE through reduced effective capacity. Slightly elevated temperatures can extend short-term TTE by lowering ohmic resistance, even though they accelerate long-term degradation. The widely held view that ``heat shortens battery life'' therefore conflates instantaneous and lifetime effects.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis}
For each multiplicative parameter $p$ we compute the dimensionless TTE elasticity
\begin{equation}
S_p = \frac{\partial \ln \mathrm{TTE}}{\partial \ln p} = \frac{p}{\mathrm{TTE}}\frac{\partial \mathrm{TTE}}{\partial p}.
\end{equation}
Fig.~\ref{fig:elasticity} ranks $S_p$ across profiles. The dominant levers are nominal capacity, screen demand, network activity, CPU load and baseline draw; flashlight, Bluetooth and RAM rank consistently low. A complementary $\pm10\%$ tornado perturbation under the gaming profile (Fig.~\ref{fig:tornado}) reaches the same conclusion through a more direct sensitivity, with screen and CPU producing the largest TTE swings.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Implications for OS Scheduling}
The sensitivity ranking maps directly to OS-level levers:
\begin{itemize}
\item Reduce persistent system services and periodic wake-ups to lower the baseline.
\item Tighten background-execution and network policies; cellular tail energy and repeated WiFi handshakes inflate the network share most visibly in idle and mixed scenarios.
\item Use energy-aware DVFS and core selection so that boosting is reserved for latency-critical work.
\item Batch network requests and coordinate cellular/WiFi switching to amortise radio wake costs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{User-Facing Recommendations}
Translating the same ranking to end users:
\begin{itemize}
\item Lower screen brightness or enable adaptive brightness, and shorten screen timeout; the display is consistently in the top three drain contributors.
\item Restrict non-essential auto-start and background apps that keep CPU and radios active.
\item Avoid running both WiFi and cellular when not needed, especially in poor-signal environments.
\item Limit sustained heavy gaming or high-bitrate streaming, both for instantaneous drain and for thermal-stress-driven aging.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}
Component coefficients are calibrated from a single device; thermal effects are lumped, and the random fluctuation model is not learned from large-scale traces. Generalising to other consumer devices (wearables, AR/VR headsets) is straightforward in form but requires per-platform calibration of $\alpha_k$ and the ECM parameters.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a continuous-time, ECM--OCV battery model with constant-power load coupling and a debounced cutoff, calibrated from public datasets and on-device ADB traces. Across four representative smartphone workloads the model yields physically interpretable TTE predictions and a clear ranking of optimisation levers: capacity, screen, network, CPU and baseline. The pipeline is reproducible from public data and portable to other consumer-electronics platforms, and provides a quantitative basis for both OS-level scheduling decisions and user-facing battery-life guidance.
